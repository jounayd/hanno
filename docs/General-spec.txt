1. SYSTEM PURPOSE AND DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
================================================================================

Project Hanno is a knowledge mining system. It uses AI language models to
systematically explore the space of ideas that current models contain but
have never produced. The core thesis is that the meaningful output space of
modern AI models is so vast that almost none of it has ever been instantiated
— and that this unexplored space contains genuinely novel ideas in mathematics,
science, and philosophy that no human has yet articulated.

The system is a prototype. Its purpose is to demonstrate three things before
any public release, fundraising, or whitepaper:

  CLAIM 1: AI models can produce candidate novel ideas at scale when properly
           directed by seed concepts and temperature variation.

  CLAIM 2: Novelty within the system can be detected automatically using
           semantic fingerprinting, preventing duplicate work across mining
           cycles.

  CLAIM 3: Domain-specific verification can filter meaningful candidates from
           noise — using formal proof checkers for mathematics, falsifiability
           analysis for science, and logical consistency analysis for
           philosophy.

DESIGN PRINCIPLES:

  Honesty over impressiveness.
    The system should never overstate what it has found. A candidate idea is
    not a discovery until it passes verification. A verified idea is not
    confirmed novel until a domain expert reviews it. The pipeline must make
    these distinctions clear at every stage.

  Fail fast and cheaply.
    The most expensive verification (Lean proof checking, expert review) must
    only run on candidates that have already passed cheaper filters. The
    pipeline is ordered strictly from cheapest to most expensive evaluation.

  Everything recorded.
    Every candidate generated, every evaluation performed, every decision made
    must be persisted. The ledger is append-only. This is the foundation of
    the future blockchain layer.

  Scope discipline.
    The MVP covers three domains: mathematics, science, and philosophy. Within
    these, the system uses only the verification methods described in this
    document. No scope expansion during MVP phase.

2. ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW
================================================================================

The system consists of eight modules arranged in a linear pipeline with
feedback loops. Data flows from seeds through excavation through filtering
through verification through storage. The cartographer reads from storage and
feeds back into seed selection.

HIGH LEVEL FLOW:

  [Seeds] → [Excavator] → [Fingerprint Engine] → [Sieve] → [Domain Verifier]
                                                               → [Ledger]
                                                               → [Cartographer]
                                                               → [Reporter]

DETAILED FLOW:

  1. Seed Management provides one or more seed concepts to the Excavator.
     Seeds come from humans, from AI generation, or from the Cartographer's
     suggestions based on unexplored territory.

  2. Excavator takes each seed and generates N candidate ideas using multiple
     prompt strategies and temperature settings in parallel. Raw candidates
     are immediately persisted to the Ledger before any filtering.

  3. Fingerprint Engine processes every candidate. It converts the candidate's
     text into a semantic hash and checks it against all previously stored
     fingerprints. Exact duplicates are rejected immediately. Near-duplicates
     are flagged for review but not automatically rejected.

  4. Sieve evaluates non-duplicate candidates across multiple dimensions:
     coherence, novelty against external literature, significance, and
     fertility. It assigns a score and a pass/fail decision. Failed candidates
     are logged with reasons.

  5. Domain Verifier applies domain-specific verification to sieve passes.
     Mathematics candidates go to formal proof checking. Science candidates
     go to falsifiability analysis. Philosophy candidates go to logical
     consistency analysis. This is the most expensive layer and only runs
     on sieve passes.

  6. Ledger records everything permanently. It is the ground truth of the
     system. All downstream processes read from the Ledger.

  7. Cartographer reads the Ledger continuously. It builds a map of explored
     idea space, identifies under-explored regions, flags bottleneck ideas,
     and generates seed suggestions for the next Excavator cycle.

  8. Reporting Engine reads from the Ledger on demand and produces structured
     reports of discoveries, statistics, and exploration maps.

FEEDBACK LOOPS:

  Loop A: Cartographer → Seed Management
    The Cartographer identifies regions of idea space not yet explored and
    generates seeds targeting those regions. This prevents the Excavator from
    repeatedly exploring the same territory.

  Loop B: Domain Verifier errors → Excavator prompts
    When the Domain Verifier rejects candidates for specific reasons (e.g.,
    Lean translation fails consistently for a category of statement), this
    feedback is used to refine Excavator prompts in that area.

  Loop C: Human review → Sieve thresholds
    After each batch of human expert review, sieve thresholds are calibrated
    based on which candidates experts found valuable versus not.

3. DOMAIN DEFINITIONS AND SCOPE
================================================================================

The MVP covers exactly three domains. Domain boundaries are strict.
The system must not attempt to evaluate ideas that fall outside these
boundaries during the MVP phase.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DOMAIN 1: MATHEMATICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition:
  Formal mathematical structures, conjectures, theorems, proofs, definitions,
  and frameworks that can be expressed in the language of formal mathematics
  and evaluated for correctness by automated proof assistants.

In scope:
  - Novel conjectures: statements believed true but not yet proven
  - Partial proofs: proof sketches with identified key steps
  - New definitions: formal definitions that generalize or unify existing
    mathematical structures
  - New frameworks: axiomatic systems or structural approaches that open
    new areas of inquiry
  - Cross-domain connections: formal identification of structural isomorphisms
    between different areas of mathematics

Sub-domains included:
  Number theory, algebra (abstract, linear, commutative), topology (algebraic,
  differential, geometric), analysis (real, complex, functional), combinatorics,
  graph theory, logic and foundations, category theory, differential geometry,
  probability theory, information theory (mathematical aspects)

Out of scope for mathematics domain:
  - Applied mathematics without novel theoretical content
  - Numerical methods or computational approaches without mathematical novelty
  - Mathematical physics (handled in science domain)
  - Statistics (unless novel theoretical result)

Verification method: Lean 4 formal proof assistant with Mathlib library
  A candidate passes mathematics verification if its formal statement can be
  expressed as a well-typed Lean 4 theorem and the statement is not trivially
  derivable from a single Mathlib lemma. Full proof is not required for MVP.
  Statement well-typedness is the primary verification target.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DOMAIN 2: SCIENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition:
  Empirical hypotheses, theoretical frameworks, predictions, and mechanistic
  explanations in natural science that are falsifiable, make testable
  predictions, and connect to existing scientific knowledge in non-trivial ways.

In scope:
  - Novel empirical hypotheses: claims about natural phenomena not yet tested
  - Theoretical frameworks: conceptual structures that unify existing findings
    or predict new phenomena
  - Mechanistic proposals: proposed causal explanations for known phenomena
  - Cross-domain scientific connections: formal identification of structural
    similarities between findings in different scientific fields
  - Quantitative predictions: specific numerical or directional predictions
    derivable from a theoretical position

Sub-domains included:
  Physics (theoretical, particle, condensed matter, cosmological),
  Chemistry (theoretical, computational, organic mechanisms),
  Biology (theoretical, evolutionary, molecular, systems biology),
  Neuroscience (theoretical, computational),
  Cognitive science (theoretical aspects),
  Ecology (theoretical, mathematical ecology),
  Materials science (theoretical aspects),
  Mathematical physics and physical mathematics

Out of scope for science domain:
  - Engineering applications without novel theoretical content
  - Clinical medicine or specific drug claims (regulatory and ethical concerns)
  - Social sciences (insufficient falsifiability for this system)
  - Economics (handled separately — too contested for automated verification)
  - Pseudoscientific claims or areas without scientific consensus basis

Verification method: Falsifiability and consistency analysis
  A candidate passes science verification if it meets all of:
    a) States at least one specific, falsifiable prediction
    b) Is not contradicted by established experimental results
    c) Connects to existing scientific frameworks non-trivially
    d) Specifies the conditions under which it would be falsified
  Verification is performed by structured AI analysis against scientific
  literature, not by actual experiment. Experimental validation is outside
  the scope of the MVP.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DOMAIN 3: PHILOSOPHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition:
  Formal arguments, conceptual analyses, logical frameworks, and philosophical
  positions in analytic philosophy that can be evaluated for logical validity,
  internal consistency, and non-triviality.

In scope:
  - Novel arguments: formally structured arguments with stated premises and
    conclusions
  - Conceptual analyses: precise definitions or decompositions of concepts
    that reveal non-obvious structure
  - Thought experiments: novel scenarios that reveal tensions in existing
    positions
  - Logical frameworks: formal or semi-formal systems for reasoning about
    philosophical problems
  - Counter-examples: specific cases that challenge established philosophical
    positions
  - Connections between philosophical positions and formal sciences

Sub-domains included:
  Philosophy of mathematics, philosophy of science, logic and formal epistemology,
  philosophy of mind (analytic tradition), metaphysics (formal approaches),
  philosophy of language, decision theory and formal ethics,
  philosophy of physics, philosophy of biology

Out of scope for philosophy domain:
  - Continental philosophy (insufficient formal structure for automated analysis)
  - Aesthetic philosophy (no falsifiability criterion)
  - Purely normative ethics without formal structure
  - Political philosophy (too value-laden for automated novelty assessment)
  - Religious philosophy

Verification method: Logical consistency and validity analysis
  A candidate passes philosophy verification if it meets all of:
    a) Premises are explicitly stated
    b) Argument form is valid (conclusion follows from premises)
    c) No obvious equivocation in key terms across premises
    d) Position is not trivially equivalent to a named existing position
    e) Engages with and differentiates from closest existing positions
  Verification is performed by structured AI analysis. Human expert review
  is mandatory before any philosophy candidate is claimed as a discovery.

4. MODULE 1: SEED MANAGEMENT SYSTEM
================================================================================

PURPOSE:
  Manages the pool of seed concepts that direct the Excavator's exploration.
  Seeds are the entry points into idea space. Seed quality directly determines
  excavation quality. The seed system is the primary mechanism for directing
  compute toward unexplored territory.

SEED TYPES:

  Type 1: Human seeds
    Submitted by human participants. Highest quality signal. These represent
    areas where human intuition suggests unexplored territory exists.
    Format: Free text description of a conceptual direction, question, or
    area of interest. Length: 10-200 words.

  Type 2: AI-generated seeds
    Generated by the system itself, either randomly exploring a domain or
    directed by the Cartographer toward unexplored regions. Lower average
    quality than human seeds but can be generated at high volume.

  Type 3: Derivative seeds
    Generated from existing discoveries in the Ledger. When a discovery is
    made, the system automatically generates seeds in the conceptual
    neighborhood of that discovery to explore adjacent territory.
    These are the highest-value seeds for compounding exploration.

SEED ATTRIBUTES:
  - Unique ID
  - Text content
  - Type (human / ai-generated / derivative)
  - Domain (mathematics / science / philosophy / cross-domain)
  - Submitter identifier (for human seeds)
  - Parent discovery ID (for derivative seeds)
  - Submission timestamp
  - Status (queued / active / exhausted / retired)
  - Fertility score (computed after use: discoveries produced / cycles run)
  - Total cycles run against this seed
  - Total candidates produced from this seed
  - Total discoveries attributed to this seed

SEED QUEUE MANAGEMENT:
  Seeds are maintained in a priority queue. Priority is determined by:
    - Fertility score (higher = higher priority, but unknown for new seeds)
    - Recency (new seeds get a priority boost for initial exploration)
    - Domain balance (system ensures no single domain dominates the queue)
    - Cartographer signal (seeds targeting under-explored regions get boost)

  A seed is marked exhausted when three consecutive cycles produce zero
  sieve passes. It is retired but never deleted.

SEED VALIDATION:
  Before entering the queue, seeds are evaluated for:
    - Minimum specificity (too vague seeds are rejected with feedback)
    - Domain assignability (must clearly belong to at least one domain)
    - Non-duplication (similar to existing seeds are flagged, not auto-rejected)
    - Appropriate scope (neither too broad nor too narrow)

  Validation is performed by AI evaluation with a structured rubric.
  Human seeds that fail validation are returned to submitter with feedback.

CROSS-DOMAIN SEEDS:
  Seeds that span multiple domains are explicitly supported and given a
  priority bonus because cross-domain exploration is where the most
  surprising discoveries are found. Examples:
    - "Connections between information entropy and topological complexity"
      (mathematics + physics)
    - "Formal accounts of emergence that satisfy both physical and
      philosophical constraints" (science + philosophy)
    - "Category-theoretic foundations for scientific theory change"
      (mathematics + philosophy of science)

5. MODULE 2: EXCAVATOR
================================================================================

PURPOSE:
  Generates large numbers of candidate ideas from seed concepts using
  AI language models. The Excavator is the engine of the system — it
  converts compute and seed concepts into raw candidate ideas.

EXCAVATION STRATEGY:
  For each seed, the Excavator runs multiple parallel generation calls
  using different combinations of:
    - Temperature settings (controlling randomness/creativity)
    - Prompt variants (different framings of the generation task)
    - Domain-specific instructions

  This multi-axis exploration is deliberate. Different temperatures and
  prompt variants access different regions of the model's output space
  for the same seed concept. High temperature produces more creative but
  less rigorous candidates. Low temperature produces more rigorous but
  potentially less novel candidates. Both are needed.

TEMPERATURE STRATEGY:
  Four temperature settings are used in each excavation run:

  Temperature 0.3 (Rigorous):
    For generating well-formed, technically precise candidates.
    Lower creativity but higher coherence.
    Best for: formal mathematical statements, precise scientific hypotheses.

  Temperature 0.7 (Balanced):
    Standard creative-analytical balance.
    Best for: general exploration across all domains.

  Temperature 1.0 (Creative):
    Higher divergence from training distribution.
    More likely to produce unusual combinations.
    Best for: cross-domain connections, philosophical frameworks.

  Temperature 1.2 (Exploratory):
    Maximum divergence within quality bounds.
    High noise rate but occasionally produces genuinely surprising outputs.
    Best for: discovering edges of idea space near the seed.

PROMPT VARIANT STRATEGY:
  Five prompt variants are used across all domains, plus domain-specific
  variants for each domain:

  Universal Variant 1 — Direct Conjecture:
    Instructs the model to state a single specific claim that is non-obvious,
    formally statable, and connects to existing knowledge in the domain.
    Emphasis on precision and formal correctness.

  Universal Variant 2 — Framework Proposal:
    Instructs the model to propose a new conceptual framework, definition,
    or structural approach that generalizes or unifies existing ideas.
    Emphasis on generativity and scope.

  Universal Variant 3 — Connection Discovery:
    Instructs the model to identify a non-obvious structural connection
    between two areas within or across domains.
    Emphasis on bridging and synthesis.

  Universal Variant 4 — Adversarial Insight:
    Instructs the model to identify something surprising, counterintuitive,
    or contrary to common assumptions that is nevertheless defensible.
    Emphasis on challenging existing positions.

  Universal Variant 5 — Open Problem Framing:
    Instructs the model to identify and precisely formulate an open question
    or problem that is not currently well-posed in the literature.
    Emphasis on question generation rather than answer generation.

  Mathematics-specific variants:
    - Proof technique proposal: novel approach to attacking a known problem
    - Counterexample construction: candidate counterexample to a known conjecture
    - Generalization: formal generalization of a known theorem

  Science-specific variants:
    - Mechanistic hypothesis: proposed causal mechanism for an observed phenomenon
    - Unification proposal: framework connecting two disconnected research areas
    - Prediction generation: specific quantitative prediction from a theoretical
      position

  Philosophy-specific variants:
    - Thought experiment: novel scenario illuminating a philosophical tension
    - Conceptual distinction: identification of a conflation in existing discourse
    - Formal argument: structured argument for a philosophical position

CANDIDATE GENERATION PARAMETERS:
  Per excavation cycle (one seed):
    - Minimum candidates: 50
    - Maximum candidates: 200 (configurable)
    - Temperature settings: 4
    - Universal prompt variants: 5
    - Domain-specific variants: 3 per domain
    - Parallel execution: yes (all calls run simultaneously where possible)

  A full cycle on one seed with 50 candidates uses approximately:
    - 50 separate API calls to the language model
    - ~10-15 candidates per temperature setting
    - ~10 candidates per prompt variant
    - Overlap between temperature and variant combinations is intentional

CANDIDATE STRUCTURE (what the Excavator must produce per candidate):
  Every candidate output must contain:
    - Domain classification (math / science / philosophy / cross-domain)
    - Core claim: the central novel assertion, precisely stated
    - Formal statement: expressed in domain-appropriate formal language
    - Intuition: non-technical explanation of why this might be true or
      interesting
    - Connection to existing knowledge: what known ideas this relates to
    - Potential significance: why this would matter if true or valid
    - Self-assessed novelty: the model's own estimate of whether this is known
    - Identified weaknesses: the model's own critique of the candidate

  The self-assessment and weakness identification are not used for filtering
  but are logged. They provide useful signal for sieve calibration.

OUTPUT QUALITY CONTROL:
  Before passing to the Fingerprint Engine, candidates are checked for:
    - Minimum length (too short = likely vacuous)
    - Maximum length (too long = likely unfocused, truncate to core claim)
    - Presence of all required fields
    - Domain assignability
  Candidates failing these checks are logged and discarded without entering
  the main pipeline. These are not counted as pipeline failures.

6. MODULE 3: FINGERPRINT ENGINE
================================================================================

PURPOSE:
  Converts every candidate into a compact semantic representation that
  captures meaning independent of surface wording. Detects when two
  candidates express the same underlying idea even if phrased completely
  differently. This is the primary anti-duplication mechanism.

  The Fingerprint Engine is the most technically novel component of the
  system. It is designed to be the core IP of Project Hanno.

FINGERPRINTING PROCESS (step by step):

  Step 1: Text preprocessing
    The candidate's core claim and formal statement are concatenated.
    Other fields (intuition, connections) are excluded from fingerprinting
    to focus on semantic content, not presentation.
    Text is normalized: whitespace standardized, symbols preserved.

  Step 2: Semantic embedding
    The preprocessed text is passed through a local sentence embedding model
    (does not require API call — runs on local hardware).
    Output: a high-dimensional vector (typically 384 or 768 dimensions)
    representing the semantic content of the idea.

    The embedding model is fixed for the lifetime of the MVP. Changing the
    embedding model invalidates all stored fingerprints and requires
    re-fingerprinting the entire ledger. This must not happen without
    a deliberate migration process.

  Step 3: Dimensionality reduction
    The high-dimensional embedding is compressed to a fixed lower dimension
    (target: 128 dimensions) using a dimensionality reduction technique.
    This compression is learned from the distribution of all fingerprints
    seen so far and must be periodically updated.

    IMPORTANT: The reduction model is not available until a minimum number
    of fingerprints exist. During the early calibration phase (fewer than
    500 fingerprints), use raw truncation of the embedding vector instead.
    After 500 fingerprints, fit the reduction model. Refit every 1000
    new fingerprints thereafter.

  Step 4: Cryptographic hashing
    The compressed vector is serialized and hashed using SHA-256.
    This produces a deterministic 256-bit fingerprint string.
    The hash is the canonical identifier for the idea's semantic content.

  Step 5: Similarity computation
    The new fingerprint's compressed vector is compared against all stored
    compressed vectors using cosine similarity.
    Cosine similarity measures the angle between vectors in the compressed
    space, not their magnitude, making it robust to length variation.

  Step 6: Collision classification
    Based on similarity score, the candidate is classified as:

    EXACT DUPLICATE (similarity >= 0.99):
      Semantically identical to an existing candidate or discovery.
      Rejected immediately. The matching existing record is logged.
      No further pipeline processing.

    NEAR DUPLICATE (similarity >= threshold, default 0.92):
      Very similar to an existing record. Flagged for human review.
      Continues through pipeline but marked with near-duplicate warning.
      Human reviewer decides whether to accept as genuinely distinct.

    NOVEL (similarity < threshold):
      Sufficiently distinct from all existing records.
      Continues through pipeline without duplication warning.

FINGERPRINT STORAGE:
  All fingerprints are stored permanently in the Ledger with:
    - The SHA-256 hash
    - The compressed vector (for future similarity comparisons)
    - The full embedding vector (for future re-compression if model changes)
    - The candidate ID it corresponds to
    - The embedding model used
    - The compression model version used
    - Timestamp

  The full embedding vector is stored even though it is large because it
  allows re-fingerprinting with a new compression model without re-calling
  the embedding model.

CROSS-DOMAIN FINGERPRINTING:
  Ideas from different domains are fingerprinted in the same space.
  This means cross-domain duplicates are also detected.
  A mathematical conjecture and a philosophical claim that express the
  same underlying structural insight will produce similar fingerprints.
  This is a feature, not a bug — it surfaces cross-domain connections.

FINGERPRINT COLLISION POLICY:
  The similarity threshold is configurable and should be calibrated.
  Starting value: 0.92
  If human review consistently finds near-duplicates that are actually
  distinct ideas: lower threshold
  If near-duplicate review finds genuinely same ideas slipping through:
  raise threshold
  The threshold is a parameter, not a fixed truth.

7. MODULE 4: SIEVE
================================================================================

PURPOSE:
  Multi-dimensional evaluation of candidates that have passed fingerprint
  checking. The Sieve filters noise from signal using a combination of
  automated AI evaluation and external literature comparison.

  The Sieve is the most expensive module after Domain Verification because
  it involves multiple AI evaluation calls per candidate. It should only
  receive candidates that have passed the Fingerprint Engine.

EVALUATION DIMENSIONS:
  Every candidate is scored on five dimensions, each producing a score
  from 0.0 to 1.0 with reasoning.

  Dimension 1: Coherence (weight: 0.25)
    Does this make sense as a statement within its domain?
    Is the terminology used consistently?
    Are the logical connectives correct?
    Are there internal contradictions?
    Domain-specific evaluation criteria:
      Math: Is this expressible as a formal mathematical statement?
      Science: Is this expressible as a testable empirical claim?
      Philosophy: Are premises and conclusion clearly distinguished?
    Score 0.0: Incoherent, uses terms incorrectly, self-contradictory
    Score 0.5: Coherent in intent but imprecise or ambiguous
    Score 1.0: Precise, unambiguous, domain-appropriate formal statement

  Dimension 2: Novelty vs. external literature (weight: 0.25)
    Is this different from existing published work?
    Evaluation method:
      a) Query external databases for related work using key terms
         from the candidate's core claim
      b) Embed returned abstracts and compare to candidate fingerprint
      c) Score based on minimum similarity to any found related work
    Score 0.0: Near-identical to a found existing result
    Score 0.5: Related to existing work but not identical; extends it
    Score 1.0: No closely related work found in databases searched
    Databases queried: arXiv (all domains), PhilPapers (philosophy),
    PubMed (science), Semantic Scholar (all domains)

  Dimension 3: Significance (weight: 0.25)
    Would this matter if true?
    Would domain experts find this interesting or important?
    Evaluation criteria:
      Does it connect to known open problems?
      Does it have implications beyond its immediate statement?
      Does it simplify or unify existing results?
      Is it non-trivially stronger than known results?
    Score 0.0: Trivially true, uninteresting, no implications
    Score 0.5: Interesting but narrow; limited implications
    Score 1.0: Potentially important; broad implications; connects to major
               open problems

  Dimension 4: Fertility (weight: 0.15)
    Does this idea open new questions?
    A fertile idea generates more questions than it answers.
    An infertile idea closes off inquiry.
    Evaluation criteria:
      How many new questions does this immediately raise?
      Does it suggest new research directions?
      Could it seed further excavation cycles?
    Score 0.0: Dead end; answers everything it raises; generative potential nil
    Score 0.5: Opens some new questions; moderately generative
    Score 1.0: Highly generative; opens multiple new research directions;
               would be an excellent seed for further excavation

  Dimension 5: Formalizability (weight: 0.10)
    Can this be expressed precisely enough for domain verification?
    This dimension is forward-looking — evaluating whether Domain Verification
    is worth attempting.
    Score 0.0: Vague; cannot be made precise without losing the point
    Score 0.5: Can be formalized with effort; some interpretation required
    Score 1.0: Already stated with sufficient precision for formal evaluation

SIEVE SCORING:
  Final sieve score = weighted sum of five dimension scores.
  Pass threshold: configurable, starting at 0.65.
  Any candidate scoring below threshold on Coherence (< 0.50) is auto-failed
  regardless of other scores — an incoherent idea cannot be a discovery.

SIEVE SEQUENCING (ordered by cost, cheapest first):
  1. Coherence check (one AI call, fast)
  2. Fingerprint collision check (already done, no additional cost)
  3. External literature novelty check (API calls to literature databases)
  4. Significance evaluation (one AI call)
  5. Fertility evaluation (one AI call)
  6. Formalizability evaluation (one AI call, last because only needed
     for candidates proceeding to Domain Verification)

  Short-circuit rule: if Coherence score < 0.50, stop immediately.
  If Literature novelty score < 0.30, stop and flag as known result.
  This prevents spending evaluation budget on clearly failed candidates.

SIEVE CALIBRATION:
  Sieve thresholds are not fixed. After each batch of human expert review,
  the following calibration process runs:

  - Compare expert judgments against sieve scores for reviewed candidates
  - Identify dimension weights that best predict expert judgment
  - Adjust weights and thresholds to improve alignment with expert judgment
  - Log all calibration adjustments with the data that motivated them

  The sieve is a living model, not a fixed algorithm.

================================================================================
8. MODULE 5: DOMAIN VERIFIER
================================================================================

PURPOSE:
  Applies domain-specific formal or semi-formal verification to candidates
  that have passed the Sieve. This is the most expensive module and should
  only receive candidates with sieve scores above threshold.

  The Domain Verifier does not determine whether an idea is true.
  It determines whether an idea is well-formed enough to be taken seriously
  by domain standards.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERIFIER 1: MATHEMATICS — LEAN 4 FORMAL VERIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT DOES:
  Translates the candidate mathematical statement into Lean 4 formal syntax
  and checks whether the statement is well-typed and expressible within
  the Lean 4 / Mathlib ecosystem.

  A passing result means: this statement is formally expressible and
  non-trivially related to existing Mathlib content.
  It does not mean: the statement is true or that a proof exists.

VERIFICATION PIPELINE:

  Stage 1: Statement extraction
    Extract the formal mathematical statement from the candidate.
    Identify all mathematical objects referenced.
    Identify the domain of mathematics (topology, algebra, etc.).
    Identify Mathlib areas likely to be relevant.

  Stage 2: Lean translation
    Translate the extracted statement into Lean 4 syntax.
    This uses AI translation with a structured prompt that:
      - Specifies required Lean 4 syntax
      - Specifies available Mathlib areas to use
      - Requires all type annotations
      - Uses sorry as proof placeholder (we are not proving, only stating)
      - Requires explicit imports
    Translation confidence is scored and stored.

  Stage 3: Type checking
    The generated Lean 4 file is submitted to the Lean 4 checker.
    The checker verifies:
      - All types are consistent
      - All referenced objects exist in Mathlib
      - The statement is well-formed
    The checker does not attempt to construct a proof.

  Stage 4: Triviality check
    If the type check passes, an additional check asks:
    Is this statement trivially derivable from a single Mathlib lemma?
    If yes: the candidate is classified as KNOWN, not novel.
    If no: the candidate passes mathematics verification.

  Stage 5: Result classification
    VERIFIED: Statement is well-typed, non-trivial
    KNOWN: Statement is a known Mathlib result
    UNTRANSLATABLE: Valid mathematical idea but cannot be expressed in
                    current Lean 4 / Mathlib (stored separately, valuable)
    MALFORMED: Translation failed due to incoherent source statement
    TIMEOUT: Lean checker exceeded time limit (retry once, then log)

KNOWN CHALLENGES WITH LEAN INTEGRATION:
  - Translation failure rate: ~50-70% on first attempt
    Mitigation: Retry up to 3 times, feeding errors back to the translator
    Mitigation: Track common failure patterns for prompt improvement

  - Mathlib compilation time: 30-120 seconds per check
    Mitigation: Use Lean server mode to maintain a running Lean process
    Mitigation: Cache compiled Mathlib state

  - Some valid mathematics has no Mathlib equivalent yet
    Mitigation: UNTRANSLATABLE is a valid and valuable outcome — it means
    the idea is potentially frontier mathematics beyond current Mathlib

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERIFIER 2: SCIENCE — FALSIFIABILITY AND CONSISTENCY ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT DOES:
  Evaluates scientific candidates for Popperian falsifiability, internal
  consistency, and compatibility with established experimental results.

  A passing result means: this hypothesis makes testable predictions,
  is not contradicted by known results, and connects to existing science.
  It does not mean: the hypothesis is true or has been tested.

VERIFICATION PIPELINE:

  Stage 1: Hypothesis extraction
    Extract the core empirical claim.
    Identify what the hypothesis predicts.
    Identify the scientific domain.
    Identify the closest existing theoretical frameworks.

  Stage 2: Falsifiability analysis
    For each prediction identified:
      - Is there a conceivable experiment that could falsify it?
      - Is that experiment feasible in principle (even if not currently)?
      - Is the prediction specific enough to be testable?
    A hypothesis with no falsifiable predictions fails immediately.
    A hypothesis with at least one specific, feasible falsification test passes
    this stage.

  Stage 3: Literature consistency check
    Query scientific databases for existing experimental results in the
    relevant domain. Compare hypothesis predictions against known results.
    Classification:
      CONSISTENT: No known results contradict the hypothesis
      PARTIALLY CONSISTENT: Some results support, some tension exists
      CONTRADICTED: Known results already falsify the hypothesis
    Contradicted hypotheses fail verification.

  Stage 4: Theoretical framework connection
    Does this hypothesis connect to existing theoretical frameworks?
    Is it derivable from, or consistent with, established theory?
    Does it explain something existing theories do not explain?
    A hypothesis that connects to no existing framework and explains nothing
    known is scientifically weak even if technically falsifiable.

  Stage 5: Novelty assessment
    Is the experimental test suggested by this hypothesis one that has
    already been run? If so, what were the results?
    If the hypothesis has already been tested and the prediction confirmed
    or disconfirmed, it is not novel — it is a rediscovery.

  Stage 6: Result classification
    VERIFIED: Falsifiable, consistent, connected, novel test implied
    CONTRADICTED: Falsified by existing experimental evidence
    REDUNDANT: Already tested and result known
    UNFALSIFIABLE: Cannot specify a falsification condition
    DISCONNECTED: Does not connect to existing scientific knowledge

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERIFIER 3: PHILOSOPHY — LOGICAL VALIDITY AND NOVELTY ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT DOES:
  Evaluates philosophical candidates for argument validity, premise
  plausibility, internal consistency, and distinctness from existing
  named philosophical positions.

  A passing result means: the argument is valid, premises are plausible,
  and the position is distinct from named existing positions.
  It does not mean: the argument is sound (premises might be false).
  Human expert review is mandatory for all philosophy candidates.

VERIFICATION PIPELINE:

  Stage 1: Argument reconstruction
    Extract the argument into standard form:
      - Numbered premises (P1, P2, ...)
      - Intermediate conclusions (IC1, IC2, ...)
      - Final conclusion (C)
    Identify all key terms and their definitions as used in the argument.
    If the argument cannot be reconstructed into standard form, it fails.

  Stage 2: Validity check
    Does the conclusion follow from the premises?
    Evaluation criteria:
      - Would the conclusion be true if all premises were true?
      - Are there hidden premises needed to make the argument valid?
      - Does the argument commit any standard formal fallacies?
    An invalid argument fails verification.

  Stage 3: Consistency check
    Are the premises mutually consistent?
    Does the candidate commit to incompatible claims across its premises?
    Inconsistent premise sets fail verification.

  Stage 4: Term consistency check
    Are key terms used with consistent meaning throughout?
    Equivocation (using the same term with different meanings) is a
    common failure mode in philosophy.
    Candidates with clear equivocation fail verification.

  Stage 5: Existing position comparison
    Compare the candidate position to the closest named existing
    philosophical positions. For each close existing position:
      - What does the candidate share with this position?
      - What specifically distinguishes the candidate from this position?
    If no distinguishing features can be identified, the candidate is
    classified as a restatement of an existing position.

  Stage 6: Engagement requirement
    Does the candidate acknowledge and respond to the strongest
    objection to its conclusion?
    A philosophical position that ignores obvious objections is
    weaker than one that addresses them.
    This dimension affects significance score but not pass/fail.

  Stage 7: Result classification
    VERIFIED: Valid, consistent, term-consistent, distinct from existing
    INVALID: Conclusion does not follow from premises
    INCONSISTENT: Premises are mutually inconsistent
    EQUIVOCATING: Key terms shift meaning across premises
    RESTATEMENT: Indistinguishable from a named existing position
    UNSTRUCTURED: Cannot be reconstructed into argument form

9. MODULE 6: LEDGER
================================================================================

PURPOSE:
  The permanent, append-only record of all system activity. The Ledger is
  the ground truth of the system and the foundation of the future blockchain.
  Nothing in the Ledger is ever modified or deleted. Every event is recorded.

LEDGER DESIGN PRINCIPLES:
  - Append-only: no updates, no deletes, ever
  - Complete: everything that happens in the pipeline is recorded
  - Timestamped: every record has a creation timestamp
  - Linked: every record references the records it depends on
  - Exportable: the entire Ledger must be exportable to JSON at any time

RECORD TYPES:

  Seeds:
    Complete record of every seed in the system.
    Status history is stored as separate events, not field updates.

  Candidates:
    Every candidate generated by the Excavator.
    Stored before any filtering — the raw output is the permanent record.
    Fields: ID, seed ID, full text, domain, temperature, prompt variant,
    model used, timestamp.

  Fingerprints:
    Every fingerprint generated.
    Fields: hash, compressed vector, full embedding vector, candidate ID,
    embedding model, compression model version, timestamp.

  Collision events:
    Every collision detection result.
    Fields: query fingerprint hash, matched fingerprint hash, similarity score,
    collision classification, timestamp.

  Sieve evaluations:
    Every dimension score, reasoning, and pass/fail decision.
    One record per candidate per sieve run.
    Fields: candidate ID, all five dimension scores, all reasoning strings,
    final score, passed boolean, timestamp.

  Domain verification results:
    Every domain verification attempt and result.
    One record per attempt (retries are separate records).
    Fields: candidate ID, domain, verification method, result classification,
    full verifier output, translation (for Lean), confidence scores, timestamp.

  Discoveries:
    The core record type. Created when a candidate passes all pipeline stages.
    Fields: ID, candidate ID, fingerprint hash, seed ID, domain, tier,
    full statement, formal statement, sieve score, verification result,
    lean-verified boolean (math only), human-reviewed boolean,
    human reviewer ID (when reviewed), creation timestamp.

  Human review records:
    Every human expert review of a discovery or candidate.
    Fields: discovery or candidate ID, reviewer ID, domain, verdict,
    detailed notes, novelty assessment, significance assessment, timestamp.

  Calibration events:
    Every threshold or weight adjustment made to any module.
    Fields: module name, parameter name, old value, new value, reason,
    supporting data reference, timestamp.

LEDGER QUERY INTERFACE:
  The Ledger exposes a read interface (not an update interface) supporting:
    - Get all discoveries (filterable by domain, tier, verified status)
    - Get discovery by ID
    - Get all candidates from a seed
    - Get pipeline statistics for a time range
    - Get all records linked to a discovery (full provenance chain)
    - Export full Ledger to JSON

10. MODULE 7: CARTOGRAPHER
================================================================================

PURPOSE:
  Builds and maintains a map of explored idea space. Uses this map to
  identify under-explored regions and generate targeted seed suggestions.
  Flags ideas that are blocking further progress in a domain.

EXPLORATION MAP:
  The Cartographer maintains a geometric representation of explored idea
  space using the fingerprint vectors of all candidates and discoveries.

  The map is organized as clusters: groups of semantically similar ideas
  that have been explored. Each cluster has:
    - A centroid (average vector of all fingerprints in the cluster)
    - A density (number of candidates and discoveries in the cluster)
    - A quality score (average sieve score of candidates in the cluster)
    - A discovery rate (discoveries per candidate in the cluster)
    - A domain label (which domain most candidates in the cluster belong to)

  The map updates after every excavation cycle.

UNDER-EXPLORED REGION IDENTIFICATION:
  A region of idea space is under-explored if:
    - It is close to an existing cluster but has low density, or
    - It is adjacent to a high-quality cluster (suggesting nearby fertile
      territory), or
    - The Lexical analysis of existing discoveries suggests adjacent concepts
      that have not yet been explored as seeds

SEED GENERATION FROM MAP:
  The Cartographer generates seed suggestions by:
    1. Identifying the centroid of under-explored regions
    2. Finding the nearest high-quality explored cluster
    3. Generating a seed that bridges from the explored cluster toward
       the unexplored region
    4. Verifying the generated seed is different enough from existing seeds

BOTTLENECK FLAGGING:
  A bottleneck is a discovery or concept that appears to block progress
  in a field — other explorations seem to depend on resolving this idea
  before they can proceed.

  Bottleneck signals:
    - Multiple separate excavation runs converge on similar ideas
      that reference the same unresolved question
    - Sieve evaluations frequently cite the same open question as a
      prerequisite for significance
    - Domain verifier notes that translation requires resolving the same
      undefined concept

  Bottleneck discoveries are:
    - Flagged with bottleneck status in the Ledger
    - Given priority as seeds for subsequent excavation
    - Highlighted in reports as high-value targets

TERRITORY STATISTICS:
  The Cartographer produces the following statistics on demand:
    - Total distinct conceptual regions explored
    - Densest region (most excavated area, potentially over-saturated)
    - Sparsest high-quality adjacent region (highest exploration priority)
    - Discovery rate by region (where has the system found the most?)
    - Cross-domain connection map (ideas that span multiple domains)
    - Bottleneck list with estimated blocking impact
    - Seed fertility leaderboard (which seeds produced the most discoveries)

13. PIPELINE FLOW
================================================================================

COMPLETE PIPELINE (one seed, one cycle):

  INPUT: One seed concept, domain, number of candidates requested

  STAGE 1 — EXCAVATION
    For each of N candidates to generate:
      Select temperature and prompt variant combination
      Call language model with seed + domain + variant prompt
      Parse and structure the raw output
      Validate required fields present
      Store raw candidate to Ledger immediately
    Output: List of N structured candidates

  STAGE 2 — FINGERPRINTING
    For each candidate:
      Generate semantic embedding (local, no API call)
      Reduce dimensions
      Compute SHA-256 hash
      Query Ledger for all stored fingerprints
      Compute cosine similarity against all stored fingerprints
      Classify: NOVEL / NEAR_DUPLICATE / EXACT_DUPLICATE
      Store fingerprint and collision event to Ledger
      If EXACT_DUPLICATE: mark candidate as duplicate, remove from pipeline
      If NEAR_DUPLICATE: mark candidate with warning, continue in pipeline
    Output: List of candidates minus exact duplicates, with collision metadata

  STAGE 3 — SIEVE
    For each non-duplicate candidate:
      Run coherence evaluation
      If coherence score < 0.50: fail, log, remove from pipeline, stop
      Run external literature novelty check
      If novelty score < 0.30: fail, log, remove from pipeline, stop
      Run significance evaluation
      Run fertility evaluation
      Run formalizability evaluation
      Compute weighted final score
      If final score < threshold: fail, log, remove from pipeline
      Store full sieve evaluation to Ledger
    Output: List of candidates that passed all sieve dimensions

  STAGE 4 — DOMAIN VERIFICATION
    For each sieve-passing candidate:
      Determine domain
      Route to appropriate domain verifier:
        Mathematics → Lean 4 verification pipeline (up to 3 attempts)
        Science → Falsifiability and consistency pipeline
        Philosophy → Logical validity and novelty pipeline
      Store complete verification record to Ledger
    Output: List of candidates with verification results

  STAGE 5 — DISCOVERY MINTING
    For each candidate with a non-failure verification result:
      Assign discovery tier based on sieve score and verification result
      Create Discovery record in Ledger
      Generate derivative seeds from this discovery
      Add derivative seeds to Seed Management queue
      Update Cartographer with new discovery
    Output: List of new Discovery records

  STAGE 6 — CARTOGRAPHER UPDATE
    Update exploration map with all candidates from this cycle
    Recompute cluster assignments
    Identify new under-explored regions
    Generate new seed suggestions
    Update bottleneck flags if new evidence
    Store updated territory statistics

  STAGE 7 — REPORTING
    Generate cycle summary report
    Update discovery report
    Flag discoveries ready for human review
    Output cycle statistics

FAILURE HANDLING:
  Every failure at every stage is:
    - Logged with full details to the Ledger
    - Counted in pipeline statistics
    - Analyzed in aggregate to identify systematic issues

  No failure causes the pipeline to stop processing other candidates.
  Failures of individual candidates are isolated.

  If a module itself fails (not a candidate failure but a system failure):
    - Log the system error with full context
    - Halt processing of that module for this cycle
    - Attempt restart once
    - If restart fails, complete the cycle without that module
      and flag the cycle as incomplete in statistics

16. RISK REGISTER
================================================================================

RISK 1: Lean translation failure rate too high to demonstrate value
  Likelihood: High
  Impact: Medium (only affects mathematics domain)
  Mitigation: Accept UNTRANSLATABLE as a valid outcome category;
              track these separately as "frontier mathematics candidates";
              reduce dependence on Lean verification for MVP claim
  Fallback: Demonstrate with human mathematician review instead of Lean

RISK 2: All sieve passes turn out to be known results on human review
  Likelihood: Medium
  Impact: High (undermines core claim)
  Mitigation: Increase diversity of seeds; lower sieve pass threshold
              initially to see more candidates; calibrate against expert
              review early and often
  Fallback: Recalibrate sieve more aggressively; the system still
            demonstrates the pipeline even if initial discoveries are weak

RISK 3: Science domain candidates consistently contradicted by literature
  Likelihood: Medium
  Impact: Low (science is harder domain for novelty; expected)
  Mitigation: Focus science seeds on theoretical/mechanistic claims
              rather than empirical predictions which are more likely
              to already be known
  Note: This is a feature not a bug — finding contradictions is
        scientifically valuable information

RISK 4: Philosophy domain produces only restatements of existing positions
  Likelihood: High initially

ADDENDUM: RESEEDING MECHANISM
================================================================================
Document Version: 2.1
Addition Date: June 2026

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PURPOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The reseeding mechanism allows the system to inject additional guidance
into an active excavation run when it detects promising signals. Rather
than completing a fixed cycle and waiting for the next one, the system
can receive new seeds mid-flight that redirect or deepen exploration
along a productive path.

This is optional. The system operates without it. But when activated,
it allows a human operator or the system itself to say: "this direction
is producing interesting candidates — go deeper here before moving on."

The analogy is a gold prospector who finds flecks of gold in a stream.
Instead of moving to the next planned location, they follow the stream
upward toward the source.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT TRIGGERS RESEEDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Reseeding can be triggered in three ways:

TRIGGER 1 — HUMAN OPERATOR (Manual)
  A human watching the pipeline output sees promising candidates emerging
  and injects additional seeds to guide the excavator deeper into that
  territory. This is purely manual, always optional, and requires
  no automation. It is the simplest form of reseeding.

  Example: The excavator is running on seed M-003 (category-theoretic
  foundations of information theory) and produces three candidates that
  all point toward a specific connection between entropy functors and
  probability monads. The operator notices this convergence and injects
  a more specific seed: "entropy as a natural transformation between
  probability monads" to focus the next generation pass.

TRIGGER 2 — AUTOMATIC SIGNAL (System-Detected)
  The system detects a statistical signal suggesting the current seed
  is in fertile territory and automatically generates and injects
  a derivative reseed without human intervention.

  Signal conditions that trigger automatic reseeding:
    a) Sieve pass rate in current cycle exceeds 2x baseline rate
    b) Three or more candidates from the current cycle share a specific
       sub-concept not present in the original seed
    c) Two or more candidates from the current cycle receive significance
       scores above 0.85
    d) A candidate from the current cycle passes domain verification
       (this is the strongest signal — always triggers a reseed)

  Automatic reseeding is conservative. It requires at least two of the
  above conditions to fire simultaneously before generating a reseed.
  A single high-scoring candidate is not sufficient alone.

TRIGGER 3 — CARTOGRAPHER SIGNAL
  The Cartographer detects that the current exploration is approaching
  a previously identified high-value region and generates a bridging
  seed to help the excavator cross into that region.

  This trigger requires the Cartographer to have sufficient map data
  to identify high-value regions — not available until after 20+
  completed cycles. Not relevant for the earliest MVP operation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW RESEEDING WORKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A reseed is not a replacement of the current seed. It is an additional
seed injected alongside the current one for a follow-up generation pass.

The mechanism works as follows:

STEP 1 — RESEED GENERATION
  When a trigger fires, a reseed candidate is generated. The reseed is
  derived from the current cycle's most promising candidates rather than
  from the original seed alone.

  Reseed generation takes as input:
    - The original seed that is currently running
    - The top N candidates from the current cycle (by sieve score)
    - The specific sub-concepts that appeared repeatedly across candidates
    - The domain verification results so far (if any)

  The reseed generator identifies:
    - What specific direction the promising candidates are pointing toward
    - What question they collectively seem to be approaching
    - What more focused seed would push deeper into that direction

STEP 2 — RESEED VALIDATION
  The generated reseed is checked for:
    - Specificity: is it more focused than the original seed?
    - Novelty: is it sufficiently different from all existing seeds?
    - Coherence: is it a valid seed concept in the domain?
  If it fails validation, reseeding is aborted for this cycle.
  No reseed is better than a bad reseed.

STEP 3 — RESEED INJECTION
  The validated reseed is added to the Seed Management queue with:
    - Priority: HIGH (above standard queue)
    - Type: derivative
    - Parent seed ID: the seed that triggered it
    - Parent cycle ID: the cycle that generated it
    - Trigger type: manual | automatic | cartographer
    - Trigger evidence: the candidates or signals that caused the reseed

STEP 4 — RESEED EXECUTION
  The next excavation pass runs the reseed as an additional seed
  alongside (not instead of) whatever seed was next in the queue.
  Both generate candidates simultaneously.

  If the reseed also produces high-signal output, it can itself
  trigger further reseeding. This creates a chain of deepening
  exploration in a productive direction.

STEP 5 — RESEED CHAIN LIMIT
  To prevent infinite recursion, each seed can only trigger a maximum
  number of reseed generations before the chain must be manually
  continued by a human operator.

  Default maximum reseed chain depth: 3
  That is: original seed → reseed 1 → reseed 2 → reseed 3 → stop.
  Further exploration of that direction requires a human decision to
  create a new seed manually.

  This limit is configurable. Setting it to 1 means only one automatic
  reseed per original seed. Setting it to 0 disables automatic reseeding
  entirely (manual trigger still available).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESEED LINEAGE TRACKING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every reseed maintains a complete lineage chain in the Ledger:

  Original seed (root)
    └── Reseed 1 (derived from original, cycle 5)
          └── Reseed 2 (derived from reseed 1, cycle 6)
                └── Reseed 3 (derived from reseed 2, cycle 7)
                      [chain limit reached — human decision required]

All discoveries produced by reseeds are linked to the full lineage chain.
Seed royalties flow through the lineage:
  - Original seed creator earns royalties on all discoveries in the chain
  - Each reseed contributor (if human) earns a smaller royalty on discoveries
    produced specifically from their reseed

This lineage tracking is the foundation for the future token royalty system.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESEED DATA MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

RESEED_EVENT (additional Ledger record type):
  id: unique identifier
  original_seed_id: the root seed that started the chain
  parent_seed_id: the immediate parent seed (may equal original or be a
                  previous reseed in the chain)
  generated_reseed_id: the new seed created by this reseed event
  trigger_type: manual | automatic | cartographer
  trigger_conditions: list of conditions that fired (for automatic triggers)
  triggering_candidates: list of candidate IDs that provided the signal
  chain_depth: integer (1 for first reseed from original, 2 for second, etc.)
  operator_id: identifier of human operator (if trigger_type = manual)
  created_at: timestamp

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONFIGURATION PARAMETERS FOR RESEEDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

reseed_enabled: boolean, default true
  Master switch. If false, no reseeding occurs regardless of triggers.

reseed_manual_enabled: boolean, default true
  Enables human operator manual reseeding.

reseed_automatic_enabled: boolean, default true
  Enables system-detected automatic reseeding.

reseed_cartographer_enabled: boolean, default false
  Cartographer-triggered reseeding. Default false until Cartographer
  has sufficient map data (recommend enabling after 20+ completed cycles).

reseed_chain_depth_max: integer, default 3
  Maximum automatic reseed chain length per original seed.

reseed_automatic_signal_threshold: integer, default 2
  Minimum number of signal conditions that must fire simultaneously
  to trigger automatic reseeding.

reseed_sieve_pass_rate_multiplier: float, default 2.0
  Sieve pass rate must exceed baseline by this multiple to count
  as a signal condition.

reseed_significance_signal_threshold: float, default 0.85
  Minimum significance score for a candidate to count toward
  the automatic reseeding signal.

reseed_significance_signal_count: integer, default 2
  Number of candidates exceeding significance threshold required
  to count as one signal condition.

reseed_verification_pass_triggers_reseed: boolean, default true
  A domain verification pass always triggers reseeding regardless
  of other conditions. Recommended to keep true — a verified discovery
  is the strongest possible signal of fertile territory.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER MODULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The reseeding mechanism touches every module but owns none of them.
It is a coordination layer that sits above the pipeline:

  Seed Management: Reseeds are injected directly into the seed queue
  with HIGH priority. Seed Management handles them identically to
  normal seeds once injected.

  Excavator: No changes required. The Excavator does not know whether
  a seed is an original or a reseed. It processes all seeds identically.

  Fingerprint Engine: No changes required. Reseeds may produce candidates
  similar to their parent cycle — fingerprinting handles this naturally.

  Sieve: No changes required. Reseed candidates are sieved identically
  to all other candidates.

  Domain Verifier: No changes required.

  Ledger: New record type (RESEED_EVENT) added. All existing record types
  gain an optional reseed_chain_id field for lineage tracking.

  Cartographer: Minor addition — the Cartographer must be aware of
  reseed chains when computing exploration maps to avoid double-counting
  territory explored by a reseed as if it were independent exploration.

  Reporting: Reseed chains are highlighted in discovery reports.
  Discoveries produced through reseeding are marked with their full
  lineage. Reseed chain performance (discoveries per chain) is tracked
  as a separate metric.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS MATTERS FOR THE BROADER SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The reseeding mechanism is important beyond its immediate function.
It demonstrates something about how the system learns:

Without reseeding, the system explores broadly but shallowly.
It covers a lot of territory but does not go deep on any discovery.

With reseeding, the system can recognize promising territory and
allocate more exploration resources to it before moving on.
This mimics how scientists actually work — following promising leads
rather than following a fixed schedule.

In the future token economy, reseeding creates an important incentive:
human operators who inject accurate, high-quality reseeds at the right
moment will earn royalties on the discoveries produced by the chain.
This creates a market for human judgment about which directions are
worth pursuing — exactly the kind of human-AI collaboration that makes
the system valuable.

The reseeding mechanism is also the clearest demonstration of the
human-in-the-loop design: the system can run autonomously, but it
is designed to be guided. A skilled human operator who understands
the domain can dramatically accelerate the system's progress by
providing timely reseeds at the right moments.

This conversation is itself an example of reseeding. The original
seed was "AI output space and mining for ideas." When that produced
interesting outputs, you injected more specific seeds: "Carthaginian
methodology," "crypto coin structure," "verifiable domains only."
Each injection deepened the exploration and produced more valuable output.
The Hanno Protocol was found through a reseed chain.

================================================================================
ADDENDUM 2: SOLID SEEDS
================================================================================
Document Version: 2.2
Addition Date: June 2026

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PURPOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A solid seed is a seed submitted by a domain expert who attaches their
professional identity and credentials to it. Unlike standard seeds which
are anonymous directions for exploration, a solid seed is a signed
intellectual pitch — an expert saying publicly: "I believe this direction
is worth mining, here is my reasoning and my credentials, and I am willing
to share the discovery rewards with miners who work it."

Solid seeds create a market for expert knowledge that did not previously
exist. A researcher who has spent years identifying a promising unexplored
direction in their field can now monetize that knowledge by packaging it
as a solid seed, attracting miners to explore it, and earning a share of
whatever is discovered.

The expert does not need to do the mining. The miners do not need to know
the domain deeply. Each brings what the other lacks. The solid seed is
the contract between them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW SOLID SEEDS DIFFER FROM STANDARD SEEDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STANDARD SEED:
  - Anonymous or pseudonymous
  - Short text description of a direction
  - No attached credentials
  - Default royalty split (defined by protocol)
  - No miner commitment mechanism
  - Miners have no information about why this direction is promising

SOLID SEED:
  - Attached to a real professional identity
  - Full research pitch with supporting documentation
  - Verified credentials in the relevant domain
  - Custom royalty split defined by the seeder
  - Miners actively choose to work it based on the pitch
  - Miners have detailed expert context for why this direction is promising

This difference matters for mining quality. A miner working a solid seed
has expert context that shapes how they configure the excavator, which
prompt variants they emphasize, and how they interpret candidate outputs.
Informed mining produces better candidates than uninformed mining.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SOLID SEED STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A solid seed consists of the following components:

COMPONENT 1 — CORE SEED TEXT
  The seed concept itself. Same format as a standard seed but expected
  to be more precise and more targeted given the expert's domain knowledge.
  Length: 50-500 words.
  Must identify: the specific direction, the domain, the sub-domain,
  and the type of discovery being sought (conjecture, hypothesis,
  framework, argument).

COMPONENT 2 — EXPERT PITCH
  A structured argument for why this direction is worth mining.
  Required sections:
    a) What is currently known: summary of the existing literature
       relevant to this seed direction
    b) What is missing: precise identification of the gap, question,
       or unexplored territory
    c) Why this gap matters: significance of finding something here
    d) Why AI mining might find it: reasoning for why the excavation
       approach is well-suited to this specific direction
    e) What a discovery would look like: description of what a valid
       discovery in this direction would need to contain or prove
  Length: 200-2000 words.

COMPONENT 3 — SUPPORTING DOCUMENTATION
  Attached documents that substantiate the pitch:
    - Relevant published papers (authored by the seeder or key literature)
    - The seeder's CV or academic profile
    - Any preliminary work, notes, or partial results the seeder has
      that informed the seed
    - Links to relevant databases, datasets, or resources miners
      should be aware of
  Format: PDF attachments stored off-chain, referenced by hash on-chain.
  Minimum requirement: at least one supporting document.
  The CV is not optional — it is the primary credentialing mechanism.

COMPONENT 4 — ROYALTY DECLARATION
  The seeder's declared share of all discovery NFTs produced from this seed.
  Format: a single percentage between 10% and 50%.
  This declaration is immutable once the solid seed is published.
  The remaining percentage goes to miners according to the protocol's
  miner allocation rules.

  Minimum miner share: 50%
  Rationale: Below 50% miner share, miners have insufficient incentive
  to work the seed competitively. The market will naturally push seeders
  toward generous splits — a seeder offering 80% to miners will attract
  more compute than one offering 50%.

  Maximum seeder share: 50%
  This is a hard protocol limit, not a suggestion.

COMPONENT 5 — SEEDER IDENTITY
  The seeder's verified professional identity:
    - Full legal name
    - Primary institutional affiliation (if any)
    - Domain of expertise (must match the seed domain)
    - Contact information (email minimum)
    - ORCID identifier (strongly recommended for academic researchers)
    - Link to public academic or professional profile

  Identity is not anonymized. Solid seeds are public claims by named
  individuals. This is the source of their credibility and the reason
  miners trust them. Anonymous solid seeds are not permitted.

COMPONENT 6 — RESEEDING RIGHTS DECLARATION
  The seeder declares whether they will provide active guidance through
  reseeds during the mining process:
    - Passive: seeder submits the seed and no further involvement
    - Active: seeder commits to reviewing promising candidates and
      injecting reseeds when the system is on a productive path

  Active seeders earn a bonus royalty percentage on discoveries produced
  from their reseeds (in addition to their base royalty from the original
  solid seed). This bonus is small (suggested: 5% of miner share) to
  incentivize active guidance without over-rewarding it.

  Active involvement is a commitment, not a right. If a seeder declares
  active but does not provide reseeds within a defined response window,
  they revert to passive status for that mining run.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW MINERS INTERACT WITH SOLID SEEDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Solid seeds are publicly listed in the seed marketplace (future component).
For MVP, they are presented to operators through the CLI and reporting system.

MINER DECISION PROCESS:
  A miner reviewing a solid seed evaluates:
    1. The seeder's credentials and track record
    2. The quality of the expert pitch
    3. The royalty split (is the miner share competitive?)
    4. The supporting documentation (do the papers support the direction?)
    5. What the seeder says a discovery would look like (is it achievable?)
    6. Whether they have the domain knowledge to configure the excavator well

  If the miner chooses to work the solid seed, they explicitly accept
  the royalty terms before their compute counts toward it.

  Miners can work multiple solid seeds simultaneously. There is no
  exclusivity requirement.

MINER CONFIGURATION ADVANTAGE:
  When working a solid seed, miners have access to the full expert pitch
  and supporting documentation. This allows them to:
    - Configure prompt variants to emphasize what the expert identified
      as the most promising angle
    - Use the seeder's referenced papers as context in prompts
    - Understand what a passing candidate should look like according
      to the expert
    - Recognize near-discoveries that a general sieve might miss

  This informed mining is a significant quality advantage over standard
  seed mining. The expert's knowledge flows into the excavation process.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ROYALTY DISTRIBUTION MECHANICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

When a discovery is minted from a solid seed, the NFT ownership is
distributed as follows:

  Seeder share: as declared (10%-50%)
  Miner share: remainder after seeder and protocol shares
  Protocol treasury: fixed 5% (taken from miner share, not seeder share)
  Validator share: taken from miner share per standard validation rules

EXAMPLE — Seeder declares 30%:
  Seeder: 30%
  Protocol treasury: 5%
  Validators: 10% (split among validators per standard rules)
  Miners: 55% (split among contributing miners)

EXAMPLE — Seeder declares 10% (generous):
  Seeder: 10%
  Protocol treasury: 5%
  Validators: 10%
  Miners: 75%

EXAMPLE — Seeder declares 50% (maximum):
  Seeder: 50%
  Protocol treasury: 5%
  Validators: 10%
  Miners: 35%

MINER SHARE DISTRIBUTION (among multiple miners):
  If one miner's candidate produces the discovery: that miner gets
  100% of the miner share.

  If multiple miners contributed candidates that were combined or
  are indistinguishable in producing the discovery: miner share is
  split equally among contributing miners.

  If many miners worked the seed but only one produced the discovery:
  the discovering miner gets the full miner share. Other miners receive
  nothing from this discovery but their compute costs are their own risk.
  This is intentional — it creates competitive mining dynamics that
  increase total compute applied to promising seeds.

SEEDER ROYALTIES ACROSS MULTIPLE DISCOVERIES:
  The seeder earns their declared percentage on every discovery minted
  from their solid seed. There is no cap on total earnings.

  A solid seed that produces 20 discoveries over two years of mining
  earns the seeder their declared percentage on all 20 NFTs.

  This is the core incentive for experts to produce high-quality solid
  seeds. Fertile seeds compound in value over time.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SOLID SEED LIFECYCLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STATE 1 — DRAFT
  Seeder is preparing the solid seed. Not visible to miners.
  All components can be modified freely.

STATE 2 — SUBMITTED
  Seeder has submitted the solid seed for basic validation.
  System checks: all required fields present, documentation attached,
  royalty within bounds, identity information complete.
  This is automated validation only — no human review of content yet.

STATE 3 — PUBLISHED
  Solid seed passes basic validation and becomes visible to miners.
  Royalty terms are now immutable — the seeder cannot change their
  declared percentage after publication.
  Core seed text is immutable after publication.
  Supporting documentation can be supplemented (not removed) after
  publication — seeders can add papers but not withdraw them.

STATE 4 — ACTIVE
  At least one miner has accepted the terms and is running the seed.
  The seeder is notified when their seed becomes active.
  If the seeder declared active reseeding involvement, their response
  window begins when the seed reaches ACTIVE state.

STATE 5 — PRODUCING
  The seed has produced at least one discovery.
  The seeder receives notification of each discovery and their NFT
  share allocation.

STATE 6 — EXHAUSTED
  Three consecutive cycles with no sieve passes.
  The seed is no longer actively mined but remains in the system.
  Seeder can submit a revised version as a new solid seed referencing
  this one, but cannot reactivate the exhausted seed.

STATE 7 — RETIRED
  Seeder voluntarily withdraws the seed before any discoveries are
  made. After retirement, the seed is archived but no longer mineable.
  Once any discovery has been made from the seed, retirement is not
  permitted — the seed remains in the system permanently.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SEEDER REPUTATION SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Seeders build reputation over time based on the fertility of their seeds.

REPUTATION METRICS:
  - Total solid seeds published
  - Total solid seeds that produced at least one discovery
  - Total discoveries produced across all solid seeds
  - Average sieve pass rate across all solid seeds (relative to baseline)
  - Expert review ratings: when discoveries from their seeds are human-
    reviewed, the reviewer can rate the quality of the solid seed pitch
    separately from the discovery itself
  - Seeder fertility score: discoveries produced per solid seed published

REPUTATION EFFECTS:
  High-reputation seeders:
    - Their solid seeds appear higher in the miner queue
    - They earn a small reputation bonus on their NFT share
      (suggested: up to 5% additional, protocol-funded)
    - They gain access to cross-seed analytics showing how their seeds
      perform relative to similar seeds from other experts

  Low-reputation seeders:
    - Their solid seeds appear lower in the miner queue
    - After 3 consecutive solid seeds that produce zero discoveries,
      they enter a probationary period where their seeds are flagged

  Note: Reputation is earned, never purchased. There is no mechanism
  to buy a higher reputation score.

FIRST SOLID SEED HANDLING:
  A seeder's first solid seed has no reputation signal.
  The quality of their CV, papers, and pitch must carry the weight.
  This is intentional — the system should be accessible to excellent
  researchers who are new to Hanno, not only those with established
  track records in the system.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RELATIONSHIP TO RESEEDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Solid seeds and the reseeding mechanism interact in an important way:

When a solid seed is in ACTIVE state and the system detects promising
signals (any reseeding trigger condition), the system first asks:
has the seeder declared active involvement?

  If YES (active seeder):
    System notifies the seeder of the promising signal.
    Seeder has a defined response window to review the candidates
    and submit a manual reseed.
    If seeder responds within the window: their reseed is injected
    with HIGH priority and earns the active bonus royalty.
    If seeder does not respond within the window: automatic reseeding
    proceeds as normal per the reseeding mechanism spec.

  If NO (passive seeder):
    Automatic reseeding proceeds immediately per the reseeding
    mechanism spec.
    Derivative reseeds generated from a solid seed inherit the
    seeder's royalty at a reduced rate (50% of the original declared
    rate) because the derivative seed is generated by the system,
    not the original seeder.

EXAMPLE — Active seeder, 30% declared rate:
  Original solid seed: seeder earns 30% of discoveries
  Derivative reseed (seeder provides): seeder earns 30% + 5% bonus
  Derivative reseed (system generates, seeder did not respond):
    seeder earns 15% of discoveries from that reseed

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SEEDER REPUTATION PORTABILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A seeder's academic reputation exists before Hanno. The system should
acknowledge this rather than pretend it does not.

For the first solid seed from a new seeder, the quality of their CV
and papers is used as a proxy for expected seed quality:

  Signal: h-index, citation count, publication count in the domain
  Signal: institutional affiliation prestige
  Signal: alignment between CV expertise and seed domain
  Signal: quality of supporting papers (cited, peer-reviewed, recent)

These signals do not give a reputation score directly. They are
displayed to miners alongside the seed so miners can make informed
decisions. The market translates external academic reputation into
mining attention without the system needing to formalize it.

Over time, Hanno reputation replaces or augments external reputation
as the primary signal for miner attention.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS CREATES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The solid seed mechanism creates something that did not exist before:

A MARKET FOR EXPERT DIRECTION

Currently, a researcher who identifies a promising unexplored direction
in their field has three options:
  a) Pursue it themselves (limited by their own time and resources)
  b) Write a grant proposal (slow, expensive, success not guaranteed)
  c) Do nothing with the insight

The solid seed creates a fourth option:
  d) Package the insight as a solid seed, attract compute from miners,
     and earn royalties on whatever is found — indefinitely

This is not consulting. The researcher does not work for hire.
This is not publishing. The researcher does not give the idea away.
This is equity in exploration. The researcher retains ownership of
the direction and earns from results.

For miners, solid seeds are a quality signal in a noisy market.
A solid seed from a respected researcher in a domain is evidence
that the direction is worth exploring — the researcher has skin in
the game through their reputation and through waiting for results.

The entire academic incentive structure currently misaligns individual
researchers from the collective progress of knowledge. Researchers
are rewarded for publishing, not for identifying what should be
explored next. Solid seeds begin to correct this misalignment by
rewarding expert direction separately from expert execution.
